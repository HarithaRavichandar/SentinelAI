### Take‑aways & Recommendations
1. **Class Imbalance** – SSH‑Brute and FTP‑Brute are severely under‑represented, leading to modest recall. Consider class‑weighting or oversampling for these classes.
2. **Benign vs. Attack Confusion** – Most mis‑predictions involve benign traffic being labeled as Port‑Scan (Class 4). Review the feature space for benign samples that resemble scanning behavior; engineered features (e.g., `Flow Duration`) may help differentiate.
3. **Feature Engineering** – The top features are largely volume‑ and timing‑based. Adding protocol‑level features (e.g., flag counts, packet payload entropy) could improve discrimination for the attack classes.
4. **Threshold Calibration** – Examine calibrated parameters (`calibrated_params_fresh.json`) and consider per‑class probability thresholds to boost recall for minority classes without sacrificing overall accuracy.
5. **Further Data Quality Checks** – Although no NaNs were detected, inspect raw traffic captures for labeling consistency, especially for FTP‑Brute where recall is low.
